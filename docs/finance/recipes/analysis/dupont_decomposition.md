# DuPont Decomposition (Why Is ROE What It Is?)

## Intent

Break return on equity into its drivers so you know WHETHER a high ROE comes
from real profitability, asset efficiency, or just leverage.

## Inputs Required

- Net income, Revenue, Total assets, Equity (and average balances if available).

## Method

Three-step DuPont:
ROE = Net margin × Asset turnover × Equity multiplier
    = (Net income / Revenue) × (Revenue / Assets) × (Assets / Equity)

- Net margin = profitability
- Asset turnover = efficiency
- Equity multiplier = leverage (Assets / Equity)

Five-step DuPont splits net margin further into tax burden × interest burden ×
operating margin, isolating how much ROE depends on cheap debt and low tax.

## Worked Example

Net income 45, Revenue 500, Assets 600, Equity 220.

- Net margin = 45/500 = 0.09
- Asset turnover = 500/600 = 0.833
- Equity multiplier = 600/220 = 2.73
- ROE = 0.09 × 0.833 × 2.73 = **20.5%** (ties to the direct calc)

Now compare two firms both at 20.5% ROE:
- Firm A: margin 0.15, turnover 0.91, multiplier 1.5 → quality earnings, low debt.
- Firm B: margin 0.05, turnover 0.80, multiplier 5.1 → ROE is a leverage story.
Same headline ROE, opposite risk profiles.

## Interpretation

- ROE driven by margin/turnover is durable and lower-risk.
- ROE driven by the equity multiplier is borrowed and reverses violently in a
  downturn — leverage amplifies losses as much as gains.
- A rising equity multiplier with flat margins is a warning, not progress.

## Red Flags

- ROE flat or rising while the equity multiplier climbs → the business is not
  improving; it is just adding debt.
- Margin falling, masked by buybacks shrinking the equity base.

## Limitations

Uses book equity, which buybacks and write-offs distort. Decomposition explains
the past; it does not predict whether the drivers are sustainable.
