# Revenue Recognition & Accrual Quality (CPA)

## Intent

Determine WHEN revenue should be booked and whether reported earnings reflect
real economic performance or accrual manipulation.

## Inputs Required

- The CONTRACT terms (deliverables, timing, payment, return/refund rights).
- Reported revenue, receivables, deferred revenue, and cash collected.
- Accrual components: change in receivables, inventory, payables, deferrals.

## Method

1. **The 5-step model (IFRS 15 / ASC 606):**
   a. Identify the contract.
   b. Identify the distinct performance obligations.
   c. Determine the transaction price.
   d. Allocate price to the obligations.
   e. Recognize revenue AS each obligation is satisfied (point in time or over
      time).
2. **Test accrual quality:** Accruals = Net income − Cash flow from operations.
   Large positive accruals (income >> cash) means earnings lean on non-cash
   recognition — lower quality, more reversal risk.
3. **Sloan ratio** = (Net income − CFO − CFI) / Total assets. Persistently high
   → accrual-heavy, lower-persistence earnings.
4. Reconcile revenue growth against receivables and deferred-revenue growth.

## Worked Example

Net income 45, CFO 30. Receivables rose 25 year-on-year while revenue rose 10.

- Accruals = 45 − 30 = **+15** (a third of profit is non-cash) → quality flag.
- Receivables growing 2.5× faster than revenue → revenue booked well ahead of
  collection; either generous terms, channel stuffing, or aggressive timing.
- A clean comparator: revenue +10, receivables +10, accruals near zero — earnings
  matched by cash.

## Interpretation

- Revenue recognized "over time" (subscriptions, construction) is legitimate but
  must track delivery; revenue pulled forward to a point in time inflates the
  current period and steals from the next.
- Deferred revenue rising is GOOD (cash in, obligation owed) — it is conservative.
  Receivables rising fast is the warning.

## Red Flags

- Revenue up, cash flow flat/down, receivables up — the classic accrual-quality
  trio.
- Bill-and-hold, channel stuffing, round-trip transactions, or recognizing the
  full multi-year contract upfront.
- Frequent "one-time" items that recur every quarter.

## Limitations

This reads quality from the outside; only the audit and notes confirm policy.
Accounting is jurisdiction-specific (IFRS vs GAAP) — name the standard, and
treat this as analysis, not an audit opinion.
