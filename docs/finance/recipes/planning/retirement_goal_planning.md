# Retirement & Goal-Based Planning (CFP)

## Intent

Estimate whether a person is on track for a funding goal (retirement,
education) and what saving/return changes close any gap — the CFP discipline of
goal-based, cash-flow planning.

## Inputs Required

- Target ANNUAL spending in retirement (today's money) and YEARS to retirement.
- Current SAVINGS, monthly CONTRIBUTION, expected real RETURN.
- Expected retirement DURATION and a safe WITHDRAWAL rate.
- INFLATION assumption (work in real terms to avoid double-counting it).

## Method

1. **Target nest egg** (capital needed at retirement):
   Nest egg = Annual retirement spending / Safe withdrawal rate
   (the 4% rule → ×25; a conservative 3.5% → ×~28.5).
2. **Future value of current savings:** FV = PV × (1 + r)^n.
3. **Future value of contributions** (annuity):
   FV = PMT × [((1 + r)^n − 1) / r] (use the periodic rate and period count).
4. **Gap** = Target nest egg − (FV savings + FV contributions).
5. If a gap exists, solve for the extra monthly saving, later retirement, or
   (cautiously) higher return that closes it.

## Worked Example

Want RM80k/year in retirement (today's money). Current savings RM200k, saving
RM2k/month, 20 years to go, 5% real return, 4% withdrawal rule.

- Target nest egg = 80,000 / 0.04 = **RM2.0m**
- FV of savings = 200,000 × 1.05^20 = **≈RM530k**
- FV of contributions = 24,000/yr × [(1.05^20 − 1)/0.05] = 24,000 × 33.07 = **≈RM794k**
- Projected total = 530k + 794k = **≈RM1.32m** → **gap ≈ RM680k**
- To close it: raising the monthly saving to ~RM3,700, or working ~5 more years,
  or a blend, gets there. (Each lever shown so the person chooses.)

## Interpretation

- Working in REAL terms (return net of inflation) means the RM80k target already
  holds purchasing power; don't inflate it again.
- The withdrawal rate is the biggest lever and the most uncertain — show the
  nest egg at 4% AND 3.5% so the person sees the sensitivity.
- A gap is information, not a verdict — present the levers (save more, retire
  later, adjust the goal), never a single prescription.

## Red Flags

- Assuming high real returns to make the plan "work" — optimism is not a plan.
- Ignoring sequence-of-returns risk (a crash early in retirement is far worse
  than the same crash late).
- Forgetting taxes, healthcare, and that the withdrawal rule is a guideline, not
  a guarantee.

## Limitations

This is planning analysis, not personalized financial advice. Real plans need
the person's full situation, tax position, and risk tolerance, and should be
reviewed with a licensed planner before action.
