# Earnings Quality

## Core Idea

Reported profit is an accrual number; survival is a cash number. An
institution can report a stable return on assets while quietly running out of
money, because accrual accounting recognizes income when it is *earned*
(contractually due), not when it is *collected*. The gap between the two is
earnings quality.

## Accrual Income Versus Cash Income

- Accrual income: interest and fees booked when due under the contract.
- Cash income: interest and fees actually received in the period.
- Earnings quality ratio: CASH INCOME / ACCRUAL INCOME. Sustained values below
  about 0.8 mean the income statement is increasingly a story about IOUs.

## Why Stable ROA Can Be A Warning Sign

A lender whose borrowers stop paying does not see income fall immediately —
it sees receivables grow. If loans are restructured or rolled over instead of
collected, interest keeps accruing (often capitalized into new principal), the
income statement stays smooth, and ROA looks *stable precisely because the
problem is being deferred*. Stability of a profitability ratio while cash
collection deteriorates is itself the red flag, not a comfort.

## The Mechanics Of Inflation Through Rollover

1. Borrower cannot pay an installment of principal P plus interest I.
2. Lender reschedules: new loan of P + I (interest capitalized).
3. Income statement: I recognized as income. Balance sheet: loan book grows.
4. Cash: zero received. ROA numerator intact, asset base bigger, risk higher.
5. Repeat each cycle: reported income compounds on uncollected income.

## What To Look At Instead

- Cash collection rate: ON-TIME COLLECTIONS / CONTRACTUAL DUE.
- Cash-adjusted ROA (see `recipes/analysis/roa_reliability_adjustment.md`).
- Growth of restructured exposures versus growth of total book.
- Interest received versus interest accrued, per segment.
- Provisioning charge versus the migration of loans into restructured status.
