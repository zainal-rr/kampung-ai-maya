# Financial Statements And How They Link

## The Three Statements

- **Income statement (P&L):** performance over a period. Revenue − expenses =
  net income. Accrual-based: revenue when earned, expense when incurred, not
  when cash moves.
- **Balance sheet:** position at a point in time. Assets = Liabilities + Equity.
  A snapshot, not a flow.
- **Cash flow statement:** cash movement over a period, in three buckets —
  Operating (CFO), Investing (CFI), Financing (CFF). The bridge between
  accrual profit and actual cash.

## How They Connect (the analyst's mental model)

1. Net income from the P&L flows into Retained Earnings on the balance sheet.
2. Net income is the starting line of the indirect cash flow statement; it is
   adjusted for non-cash items (depreciation) and working-capital changes to
   reach CFO.
3. The change in the cash line on the balance sheet equals CFO + CFI + CFF.
4. Every balance-sheet account ties to a flow: PP&E moves by capex (CFI) and
   depreciation; debt moves by borrowing/repayment (CFF); equity by net income,
   dividends, and share issuance.

If a "model" or analysis cannot trace those links, its numbers are not
trustworthy. The three statements must articulate.

## Accrual vs Cash (why both matter)

Profit is an opinion; cash is a fact. A company can be profitable and insolvent
(growing receivables, no collection) or cash-rich and unprofitable (deferred
revenue, heavy prepayments). Statement analysis always reads the P&L and the
cash flow together — see `recipes/analysis/cash_flow_quality.md`.

## Common-Size Statements

Express every P&L line as a percent of revenue, and every balance-sheet line as
a percent of total assets. This strips out size and makes trends and peer
comparisons legible — a 2-percentage-point creep in cost of goods is invisible
in absolutes and obvious common-sized.

## What Standards Govern (CPA lens)

IFRS and US GAAP differ in detail (inventory LIFO, development cost
capitalization, lease treatment) but share the core: accrual, matching,
prudence, and disclosure. When a recipe depends on a standard-specific
treatment, name the standard; do not assume one jurisdiction.
